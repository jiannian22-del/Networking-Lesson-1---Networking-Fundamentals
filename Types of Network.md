# Module 2 — Types of Networks

This module covers the different types of computer networks, their characteristics, examples, and the differences between the Internet and an intranet.

## Learning Objectives

- Understand different types of computer networks.
- Identify LAN, WAN, WLAN, PAN, and MAN.
- Compare network types based on coverage area and usage.
- Differentiate between the Internet and an intranet.

---

## 1. PAN (Personal Area Network)

**Definition:** A Personal Area Network (PAN) connects personal devices over a very short distance, usually around one individual.

### Characteristics
- Covers the smallest geographical area.
- Connects personal electronic devices.
- Commonly uses Bluetooth or USB.
- Usually intended for personal use.

### Examples
- Smartphone connected to Bluetooth earbuds.
- Smartwatch connected to a smartphone.
- Laptop connected to a personal device.

---

## 2. LAN (Local Area Network)

**Definition:** A Local Area Network (LAN) connects computers and devices within a small geographical area, such as a home, school, or office.

### Characteristics
- Covers a small geographical area.
- Usually provides high-speed communication.
- Uses Ethernet cables or wireless connections.
- Commonly managed by an individual or organization.

### Examples
- School computer laboratory.
- Home computer network.
- Office network.

---

## 3. WLAN (Wireless Local Area Network)

**Definition:** A Wireless Local Area Network (WLAN) connects devices within a limited area using wireless technology, typically Wi-Fi.

### Characteristics
- Covers a local area using wireless signals.
- Allows mobility within wireless coverage.
- Commonly uses wireless routers or access points.
- Requires appropriate security settings.

### Examples
- Home Wi-Fi.
- School Wi-Fi.
- Wireless office network.

---

## 4. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

**Definition:** A Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) connects multiple networks across a city or metropolitan area.

### Characteristics
- Covers a larger area than a typical LAN.
- Usually covers less territory than a WAN.
- Connects buildings, campuses, and offices within a city.
- May use high-speed fiber-optic infrastructure.

### Examples
- A university connecting campuses across a city.
- A city government connecting multiple offices.
- An organization connecting branches within a metropolitan area.

---

## 5. WAN (Wide Area Network)

**Definition:** A Wide Area Network (WAN) connects multiple networks over a large geographical area, such as cities, countries, or continents.

### Characteristics
- Covers the largest geographical area among these network types.
- Connects multiple LANs and other networks.
- Uses telecommunications infrastructure.
- May rely on network service providers.

### Examples
- A company connecting offices in different countries.
- A bank connecting branches nationwide.
- The Internet.

---

## 6. Internet vs. Intranet

### Internet

The **Internet** is a global network of interconnected networks that enables people and devices worldwide to communicate and share information.

**Examples:**
- Visiting public websites.
- Sending emails using online services.
- Watching online videos.

### Intranet

An **intranet** is a private network used by an organization to share information and resources with authorized users.

**Examples:**
- An internal employee portal.
- A school staff information system.
- A company's private document-sharing platform.

### Comparison Table

| Feature | Internet | Intranet |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Generally publicly accessible | Restricted to authorized users |
| Coverage | Global | Usually within an organization |
| Purpose | Global communication and information sharing | Internal communication and resource sharing |
| Security | Depends on services and security controls | Typically protected by access controls |
| Example | Public websites | Company employee portal |

## 7. Network Types Comparison

| Network Type | Full Form | Coverage Area | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| LAN | Local Area Network | Small area | School computer lab |
| WAN | Wide Area Network | Large geographical area | International company network |
| WLAN | Wireless Local Area Network | Local wireless coverage | Home Wi-Fi |
| PAN | Personal Area Network | Around an individual | Bluetooth earbuds |
| MAN | Metropolitan Area Network | City or metropolitan area | City-wide campus network |

---

## 8. Quick Revision Notes

- **PAN:** Connects personal devices over short distances.
- **LAN:** Connects devices within a limited geographical area.
- **WLAN:** Provides wireless connectivity within a local area.
- **MAN:** Connects networks across a metropolitan area.
- **WAN:** Connects networks across large geographical areas.
- **Internet:** A global network of interconnected networks.
- **Intranet:** A private network for authorized organizational users.

## 9. Practice Questions

1. What does LAN stand for?
2. Which network type connects offices across different countries?
3. What is the difference between LAN and WLAN?
4. Which network type commonly connects Bluetooth earbuds to a smartphone?
5. What is the difference between MAN and WAN?
6. Give two examples of a LAN.
7. Explain the difference between the Internet and an intranet.
8. Which network type can connect several university campuses across a city?

### Answers

1. Local Area Network.
2. WAN.
3. LAN describes a local network, while WLAN provides local network connectivity wirelessly.
4. PAN.
5. MAN typically covers a metropolitan area, while WAN covers larger geographical areas.
6. A home network and a school computer laboratory network.
7. The Internet is a global network of interconnected networks, while an intranet is a private network for authorized users.
8. MAN.

---

## 10. Key Takeaways

- Computer networks can be classified by their coverage area and connectivity.
- LANs cover small areas, while WANs cover large geographical areas.
- WLANs provide wireless local connectivity.
- PANs connect personal devices over short distances.
- MANs connect networks across metropolitan areas.
- The Internet is global, while an intranet provides private organizational access.
